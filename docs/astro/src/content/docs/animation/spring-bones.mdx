title: Spring bones
description: Secondary motion for chains of bones (hair, tails, cords): a SpringBone on the chain's root, SpringBoneCollider shapes to keep it out of, and what it costs.
---

Hair, tails, scarves and cords move after the body does: they lag it, swing, and settle. **Spring bones** add that to a skeleton without an animator
authoring it. A `SpringBone` on the **root** of a chain makes every entity below it a spring: the animation still says where each bone would be, and
the chain trails that pose by inertia, falls with gravity, and is pulled back toward it. The root itself is not simulated; it is what the animation or
the body drives.

It is core, not an optional module, and it runs in C++ in the engine, so it costs the same on every target, including ones with no JIT.

## The component

Put a `SpringBone` on the top bone of a hair strand (its parent bone is the body; its children are the strand). Everything below it, down every branch,
is simulated.

| Field | What it does |
|---|---|
| `enabled` | Off leaves the chain exactly as the animation poses it. |
| `stiffness` | How hard each bone is pulled back toward its animated pose, 0 to 1 a frame at 60 Hz. 0 hangs free. |
| `drag` | How much of its speed a bone loses each frame, 0 to 1. Higher settles sooner; the default is damped enough not to ring. |
| `gravity` | World units a second squared (world y is up). The default is a metre a hundred units. |
| `endLength` | A leaf bone has no child to aim at; this gives it a tip that long. 0 leaves leaf bones unsimulated. |
| `radius` | The thickness of the strand, kept clear of the colliders. |
| `weight` | How much of the simulation is used, 0 to 1. 0 is the animation alone. |
| `colliders` | Entities with a `SpringBoneCollider` the chain is kept out of. |

A `SpringBoneCollider` is a sphere, or a capsule along its entity's own Y axis, placed by the entity's transform (and its `offset`). Put them on the head
and shoulders bones so hair does not pass through the body.

## What it does each frame

The animation runs first and writes the bones' local rotations. Then, for each chain:

1. The pose the animation gave is worked out down the chain from the root, using the animation's own rotations. (A bone this system turned last frame
   and that nothing animated this frame is read as the animation had it, not as it was left, so a chain never springs toward its own last result.)
2. The chain is stepped toward that pose: each bone keeps its velocity less drag, falls with gravity, is pulled toward its pose, is put back at its length
   from its parent (parents first, so a bone cannot stretch), and is pushed out of the colliders. The time is split into steps of at most a 60 Hz frame.
3. Each bone is turned to aim at where its child has got to. **Only rotations are written**; a bone's position is never changed, so it cannot leave its parent,
   and skinning needs nothing else.

A jump of the root by more than a few times the chain's length, or a stall of half a second, puts the chain back at its pose instead of whipping it.
Switching the component off, or removing it, gives the bones back as the animation had them. `SpringBoneRes` (`app.getResource(SpringBoneRes)`) has
`reset(root)` for a teleport you want to mark yourself.

## Cost

The solver is a few arithmetic operations per bone. Measured in the editor's play realm, in the wasm engine: **three hundred chains of six bones (2100 nodes)
cost 0.28 ms an update.** The test is `springbone` in the editor checks; `test_springbone` is the C++ unit test of the solver and the system.

## Limits

- A bone with several children aims at its first child; the others follow by being children, and aim at their own.
- Scale is taken from the bones' composed scale; non-uniform scale down a chain is not modelled.
- Collision is bone against sphere or capsule: no collision between strands, no self-collision, and no collision with the world's physics shapes.
- There is no wind yet, and no authoring in the viewport: chains and colliders are set up in the Details panel. A hair importer that finds the strands and makes the chains
  from the model's bone names does not exist yet either.
