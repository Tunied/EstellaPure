---
title: Terrain
description: A Terrain component that makes ground from a heightmap or noise, paints it by height and slope, and draws it in chunks that coarsen with distance.
---

Ground is the largest thing most 3D scenes draw and the thing a mesh is worst at: too many triangles near the camera
and too few where it matters. The `Terrain` component makes it from a grid of heights and cuts it into chunks, each built at
several levels of detail, so a hill underfoot is drawn finely and the same hill on the horizon is a few hundred triangles.

## The component

Add a `Terrain` component to an entity. It is centred on that entity; its children are the chunks, made when the scene runs
(and in the editor's scene view) and rebuilt when a field changes. Nothing is baked and nothing is saved besides the component.

| Field | What it does |
|---|---|
| `heightmap` | An image: the red channel is the height. If the green channel is not just grey it is taken as the low byte of a 16-bit height, so a 16-bit map exported into an 8-bit-per-channel PNG keeps its precision. Empty makes the ground from noise. |
| `sizeX`, `sizeZ`, `height` | World size of the terrain and the world height of its highest ground. |
| `chunks` | Chunks along each side. Each is culled and drawn on its own, so more chunks cull better and cost more draws. |
| `chunkCells` | Cells along a chunk's side at its finest level. |
| `lodLevels`, `detail` | How many levels each chunk has (each half as fine as the one before) and a multiplier on how far the finer levels reach. |
| `seed`, `frequency`, `octaves`, `persistence`, `ridged`, `resolution` | The noise ground: another seed is another ground; `ridged` goes from rolling hills to sharp ridges. |
| `splatmap` | An image whose four channels are the weights of the four layers. Empty paints by height and slope. |
| `rockSlope`, `dirtHeight`, `snowHeight` | How steep ground must be to turn to rock, and the heights where grass gives way to dirt and where snow begins. |
| `layerColor0`–`3` | Grass, dirt, snow, rock: the colours the ground is drawn in when it has no material. |
| `material` | A material on the **Terrain** shader (below). Empty draws the layer colours straight onto the ground. |

## Layers and the Terrain material

The four layers are grass, dirt, snow and rock. Each vertex is given its weight of every layer, from the splatmap or from how high
and how steep the ground is there, and the **Terrain** shader (`builtin:terrain`) draws them: `u_layer0`–`3` are the textures,
`u_tint0`–`3` their colours and `u_tile0`–`3` how many world units one repeat covers. The textures are tiled by *world position*, so a
layer runs unbroken from one chunk to the next, and the rock is projected from three sides so a cliff is not a smear. `u_sharpness`
hardens the seams and `u_macroVariation` lays a slow variation over the grass so a meadow is not one repeated tile.

## How the levels of detail work

Each chunk is a grid of `chunkCells` cells a side, built again at half that, a quarter, and so on down to two cells. The engine's own
[level of detail](/docs/performance/level-of-detail/) picks among them by how large the chunk is on screen. Two neighbours at different levels
have different numbers of vertices along their shared edge, which would leave a crack; each chunk therefore has a **skirt**, a wall let
down from its edges, so a chunk is built alone and may change level without rebuilding anyone else. Heights and normals are read from the one field
at every level, so a chunk keeps its lighting as it coarsens and only its silhouette changes.

## From code

The ground can be asked how high it is, for putting things on it:

```ts
import { TerrainsRes } from 'esengine';

const terrains = app.getResource(TerrainsRes);
const y = terrains.heightAt(x, z);       // world height, or null where there is no terrain
const n = terrains.normalAt(x, z);       // the unit normal there, or null
```

## Limits

- **No collision.** The ground is drawn, not simulated: `heightAt` is the way to stand something on it, and a physics body falls through it
  unless the project adds its own collider.
- **Heights are one field.** Splat weights are per vertex, at the finest level's resolution, so they are as fine as the grid is.
- **No holes, caves or overhangs.** A heightfield has one height at each place. Caves and bridges are meshes placed with it.
- **No rotation in `heightAt`.** The terrain's position and uniform scale are taken into account, a rotation about y is not.
- **Chunks are built a few a frame**, so a large terrain appears over a moment rather than stalling one frame.
